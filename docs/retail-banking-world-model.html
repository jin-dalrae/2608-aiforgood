LedgerWorld

A world model for retail banking and credit cards.

Capital markets have simulators. Retail banking does not — because retail banking has to simulate people. Paychecks, rent, a card declined at the grocery store, the slow slide into delinquency. LedgerWorld generates the event-level life of a household under a policy the bank has not tried yet, so the experiment runs in a simulator instead of on customers.

What it isA governed, generative simulator of the household financial lifecycle
DeploymentRuns inside the bank's own VPC — no raw data leaves

02The problemAn experiment graded 180 days late

Every credit policy decision is an experiment run on real people.

Delayed truth

Defaults surface 90–180 days after the decision that caused them. Churn is slower still. By the time the outcome lands, the macro conditions that produced it are gone.

Costly exploration

Champion/challenger tests buy information with real losses and real customers. The policies worth knowing about — line cuts in a downturn — are the ones nobody dares to test.

No counterfactual

A backtest can only score what happened. It cannot say what a customer would have done under a different limit, because that spending was never recorded.

Five mature tools. None of them simulates a household.

The gap is not a better PD model. The gap is a calibrated generative simulator of the financial lifecycle, governed well enough that model risk will accept it as decision support.

04AnatomyState · Action · Transition · Reward

A controlled stochastic process over accounts, merchants, and macro climate.

05ArchitectureHybrid, not one big neural net

Accounting is enforced. Only behavior is learned.

LayerRoleWhy it is built this way
L0  LedgerDeterministic posting engine — balances, cycles, interest, min-pay, aging.Non-negotiable. Learn the ledger and balances drift; model risk rejects it on sight.
L1  HouseholdsCalibrated microsimulation — income, non-discretionary burn, liquidity-constrained payment, substitution.Interpretable and stress-stable. Runs a million accounts on CPU, and answers the MVP question on its own.
L2  ResidualEvent transformer correcting spend intensity by category, payment propensity, churn hazard, merchant choice.Captures the habit the structural layer misses. Shrunk toward zero, and always decoded through L0.
L3  ShocksMacro to regional to household — unemployment, CPI, medical and job-loss mixtures.Keeps exogenous climate separate from the policy being tested.
L4  PolicyDeclarative issuer rules in v1; constrained RL later, as a consumer of the environment.The learner never sits inside the kernel. That boundary is what keeps v1 reviewable.
Runtime, one day
structural rate×learned correction→sample events→post to ledger→daily close
Why not one giant transformer

A next-event model will not enforce a credit limit, statement math, or a 90-day default lag. It earns its place as a correction term and a synthetic decoder — never as the accounting.

Two customers can hold the same credit score while one of them is three weeks from empty. The score cannot separate them. The stream can — cash buffer, burn rate, and where this card sits in the wallet are read straight off the events.

02 — Where the money goes when the card says no
limit reached—→ authorization declined—→ competitor card cart abandoned pay-later at checkout retry same card

Each branch writes a different future. The competitor branch re-ranks the wallet permanently and costs interchange for months; abandonment keeps the relationship but suppresses spend. A model that only predicts default sees neither.

07Action spaceLevers, objective, constraints

What the bank may pull, and what counts as winning.

Line management

Limit increases and cuts, and freezes with a duration. The cuts matter most: they are the policies that are effectively untestable in production today.

Timing and hardship

Proactive alerts, balance-transfer offers, restructuring — placed before delinquency, where the simulation says the household is weeks from a shortfall.

Pricing and rewards

APR moves, category multipliers, authorization tightness. Measured on risk-adjusted lifetime value, not thirty-day spend lift.

R_t = NIM + interchange − credit losses − churn − operating cost // dollars, not a score subject to fair-lending constraints credit appetite max line change per period operational feasibility

The constraint line is not decoration. Optimize the reward alone and the policy finds the same shortcuts a bad underwriter would. Constraints live inside the optimizer, with the trade-off curve written down — and latent embeddings are never used as a reason for declining anyone.
